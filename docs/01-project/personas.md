# User Personas

## Overview

Iris serves users with different needs, responsibilities, and levels of familiarity with community services. The personas below represent the main user types considered when designing the platform.

The platform is designed around three primary groups:

- **Beneficiaries** who browse, reserve, and collect available goods.
- **Businesses and partner organizations** that publish surplus goods and manage pickups.
- **B.A.D.R. staff** who oversee beneficiary cases, eligibility, distribution, and program operations.

These personas help guide interface decisions, user flows, accessibility considerations, and the design of the platform's beneficiary, partner, and staff experiences.

---

## Beneficiary Personas

Beneficiaries are individuals and families with limited financial resources who use Iris to access available food and other essential goods. Different household situations, language needs, schedules, and familiarity with community services can significantly affect how they interact with the platform.

## How the Personas Inform the Design

The personas are used throughout the design process to ensure that Iris supports the needs of each major user group.

They influence decisions such as:

- What information is shown on goods listings.
- How reservations and pickup instructions are communicated.
- How simple and efficient the partner listing workflow should be.
- How multilingual and AI-assisted guidance can support users who face language barriers.
- How status information is communicated without relying only on color.
- How B.A.D.R. staff review information and understand decisions or cases requiring attention.
- Which information and actions should be prioritized on mobile versus staff-oriented interfaces.

The personas also provide context for the user flows and wireframes developed for each part of the platform.
