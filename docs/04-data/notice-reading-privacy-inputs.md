# Notice reading: Law 25 evidence for the privacy impact assessment

This page holds the evidence that spike SET-19 ([#410](https://github.com/B-A-D-R-SL/iris/issues/410)) gathered for B.A.D.R.'s privacy impact assessment, about two services that could read notices of assessment: Azure AI Document Intelligence and Gemini on Vertex AI in Montréal. It is not a legal opinion and not the assessment itself: B.A.D.R.'s privacy officer, [Nadia Mirsraoui](../01-project/overview.md#people), makes the legal call. All sources were read on 2026-10-23, except the Azure region check and the Google documentation pages, read on 2026-10-30 (see [Sources](#sources)). The spike sends the mock notices and the public redacted sample (no personal data) to the services, and never a real notice.

## How to read this page

- There is one row per question. The `Law 25` column names the sections of the Act (P-39.1) that the question relates to; "¶2" means the second paragraph of a section. Their wording is under [What the Act says](#what-the-act-says).
- Each provider cell starts with a status in bold. Then comes a short quote from the provider's own documents or contract, and a link with the date we read it. Longer explanations are in [Notes](#notes), numbered by row.
- Status meanings:
  - **Meets**: the provider's own documents state the fact the row asks about. It never means "Law 25 is satisfied".
  - **Partly**: the documents state part of it.
  - **No**: the option does not have it.
  - **Unknown**: the sources we could read do not answer it. We did not guess.
  - **Legal review**: the facts are in the notes, but whether they are enough under Law 25 is for the privacy officer to decide.
- Terms used on this page:
  - **DPA**: the Microsoft Products and Services Data Protection Addendum, Microsoft's contract terms for the data it processes for a customer.
  - **CDPA**: Google's Cloud Data Processing Addendum, Google Cloud's equivalent contract terms.
  - **Geo**: Microsoft's word for a large geographic area in which it keeps customer data at rest. For Iris it is Canada, which holds the Canada East and Canada Central regions.
  - **Multi-Region**: Google's word for a location made of several regions. Montréal (`northamerica-northeast1`) is a single region, not a Multi-Region.
  - **Entra ID**: Microsoft Entra ID, Microsoft's sign-in and identity service (formerly Azure Active Directory). It lets an application sign in to a service without an API key.
  - **Private endpoints**: a private network address for a service inside the customer's own Azure network, so requests do not travel over the public internet.
  - **FIPS 140-2**: a US government standard for checking that encryption modules work as specified.

## Notes

1. **Where processing happens.**
   - Azure: Iris plans to use the Canada East region ([ADR 0001](../adr/0001-tech-stack.md)), but Document Intelligence is not offered there. On 2026-10-30 the project owner checked two places. The [Document Intelligence pricing page](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/document-intelligence/) lists prices for Canada Central, the same as for West US (Layout: "$10 per 1,000 pages"), and Canada East cannot be selected. The Azure portal's form for creating a Document Intelligence resource offered Canada Central as the only Canadian region. So Document Intelligence would process notices in Canada Central, in Toronto, outside Québec ([Azure regions list](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/reliability/regions-list)), while the rest of Iris runs in Canada East. See note 2 and the s. 17 section.
   - Azure: features in preview are different. The [Microsoft DPA](https://www.microsoft.com/licensing/docs/documents/download/MicrosoftProductandServicesDPA%28WW%29%28English%29%28May2026%29%28CR%29.docx) says previews "may employ lesser or different privacy and security measures" and that "Location of Customer Data does not apply to Previews". Iris should use only generally available versions of the service.
   - Gemini: the team uses only the Montréal regional endpoint `northamerica-northeast1`, never the global endpoint, which "may be processed in any Google Cloud location around the world, and therefore don't provide any data residency guarantees" ([Data residency](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/resources/data-residency), read 2026-10-30). For a regional endpoint the same page promises that "ML processing remains entirely within the broader multi-regional or country jurisdiction associated with that region (for example, requests to us-central1 are processed within the United States)". For Montréal that jurisdiction is Canada: processing may happen anywhere in Canada, including outside Québec. The page refers to a table of "the exact ML processing commitments for each model and location"; its marks are icons, which neither our tools nor a copy of the page could read, so whether Gemini 3.5 Flash has the commitment in Montréal is not confirmed. On the contract side, Google's data residency list covers "Generative AI on Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform (only for models listed in the "ML processing" section of the Generative AI on Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform documentation and excluding Grounding with Google Search, Web Grounding for Enterprise, Grounding with Google Maps, and RAG Engine)" ([Data Residency services list](https://cloud.google.com/terms/data-residency), read 2026-10-30), so that table decides whether the location commitment is contractual for this model. Google's page on endpoints adds: "Endpoints don't guarantee data residency or in-region ML processing" ([Deployments and endpoints](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/learn/locations), read 2026-10-30). The spike did reach `gemini-3.5-flash` through the Montréal endpoint, which shows the model is offered there, not where it runs.
   - Gemini: Google's terms now use the new name "Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform API (formerly Vertex AI API)" ([Service Specific Terms §31(a)](https://cloud.google.com/terms/service-terms)). Documents may use either name.

3. **Retention and deletion.**
   - Azure: the Delete Analyze Result API "permanently deletes the submitted input data and analyze result associated with the request" ([Document Intelligence privacy page](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/responsible-ai/document-intelligence/data-privacy-security)). Iris can call it right after reading a notice.
   - Gemini: deleting data through the services is an instruction to delete it "within a maximum period of 180 days" ([Google CDPA §6.1](https://cloud.google.com/terms/data-processing-addendum)). Two optional features keep data longer: Grounding with Google Search keeps logs "for up to three (3) days", and Grounding with Google Maps stores prompts "for thirty (30) days" ([Service Specific Terms §20(k), §20(m)](https://cloud.google.com/terms/service-terms)). Iris does not need either feature.
   - Gemini: Google caches prompts and answers by default, in two tiers. In-memory caching is "stored only in memory, not at rest", "deleted no later than 24 hours after it's written" and "processed in the region that serves the request"; durable caching is "stored at rest and encrypted with Google default encryption", also deleted within 24 hours, and "stays within the selected location" for a regional endpoint. "Turning off caching disables both in-memory caching and durable caching"; it is a per-project setting (`cacheConfig` with `disableCache` set to true) that needs the project's admin role ([Zero data retention](https://docs.cloud.google.com/vertex-ai/generative-ai/docs/vertex-ai-zero-data-retention), read 2026-10-30). Turning it off is Gemini's counterpart to Azure's delete call, and Iris should do it.

Some features add logging, and "Zero data retention may not be possible when using some Advanced AI features." Caching is covered in note 3.
   - Gemini, from the [Abuse monitoring](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/abuse-monitoring) page (updated 2026-10-30; copied from the live page by the project owner). Gemini 3.5 Flash is named neither as a "Google Frontier Model" (only "Gemini 4 Argon (Cyber Permissive)") nor as "Advanced AI", so the rules for other Google models apply:

This data won't be used to train or fine-tune any AI/ML models."

## Same for both options

These duties apply whichever service is chosen.

- **s. 3.3, privacy impact assessment.** B.A.D.R. assesses Iris as a whole, and must consult its privacy officer "from the outset of the project".
- **s. 8, what households are told.** When Iris collects a notice, it tells the household the purposes, the means, and their rights. It also names the categories of third persons who receive the data (here, the AI reading provider) and "the possibility that the information could be communicated outside Québec". The current consent text, `consent_ai_reading` in the [glossary](../01-project/glossary.md), says only "I agree that software reads my notices of assessment to fill in the fields". It will be updated in US-22.
- **ss. 12 to 14, consent.** Iris treats notices as sensitive personal information, because they show the income of low-income households. Consent to sensitive information must be given "expressly". It must be "clear, free and informed", given for specific purposes, and, in writing, "presented separately from any other information". Iris asks in its own consent step. Whether s. 18.3 lets B.A.D.R. send notices to a provider without consent is a **Legal review** question; Iris asks for consent either way.
- **s. 12.1, automated decisions.** The team treats automatic approval as a decision "based exclusively on an automated processing". Iris says so when it gives the decision. On request, it must explain the personal information used and "the reasons and the principal factors and parameters", and let a staff member review the decision. The team's business rules already plan an approval email that says the decision was automated and offers a review by a person.
- **s. 11, keep decision inputs.** The information used to make a decision is kept "for at least one year following the decision". The team's plan is that Iris keeps these decision inputs in its own records, so it does not rely on the provider's copy.
- **s. 23, destroy when done.** When the purpose is achieved, Iris must "destroy the information, or anonymize it". The provider's copy should be deleted as soon as the notice is read (row 3).

### Why a data centre in Québec may not avoid s. 17

s. 17 starts with communicating information "outside Québec". Its third paragraph adds: "The same applies where the person carrying on an enterprise entrusts a person or body outside Québec with the task of collecting, using, communicating or keeping such information on his behalf." So the location of the data centre is not the only test: who is entrusted also counts. Neither option keeps processing in Québec anyway: Document Intelligence runs in Toronto, and Google commits Montréal-endpoint processing only to Canada (note 1). For Gemini, the company entrusted would be Google Cloud Canada Corporation, in Toronto, outside Québec (note 6); for Azure, it was not confirmed. Both contracts also allow processing in other countries (row 8). B.A.D.R. should plan a s. 17 assessment and written agreement for either option. **Legal review.**

| s. 10 | "security measures necessary to ensure the protection of the personal information [...] that are reasonable given the sensitivity of the information" |

The communication of the information must be the subject of a written agreement" |
